What it is

Week one done right, month three

Launches fail in the first fortnight or not at all. The listing that isn't indexed, the Vine that started late, the ads that sold the ASIN out in nine days: each is a wound the account carries for a quarter.

So we plan backwards from the stock landing date. Copy, images and backend terms locked before the cartons move. Enrolment dates on the calendar. Campaign structure built and funded before day one. At day 90 you get the playbook and the decision rules for the next quarter.

Questions

Before you print the cartons

How much stock should a launch start with?

Enough for the plan velocity plus one reorder cycle of buffer. Launching with three weeks of stock and strong ads is how ranks die in month two. We size the first order from the plan, not from the supplier minimum.

How does Vine fit in?

Vine enroles up to 30 units per parent ASIN with reviews from Amazon trusted reviewers, timed to land in weeks one and two of trading. It is the only review accelerator we use, because it is the only one that is fully by the book.

When is the right time to expand into the EU?

When the UK account has repeatable rank and stock discipline, and someone can own VAT and compliance properly. Brands that expand to escape a problem import the problem.

How much ad spend does a launch need?

Enough to hold page one on the mapped terms through the review-building window, usually six to ten weeks. The plan states the number before you commit, and ads pace to stock so the ASIN never sells out mid-climb.

What is in the handover playbook?

The launch calendar, campaign structure, keyword map, reorder points, review cadence and the decision rules for the next 90 days: everything a team needs to run the ASIN without us, or a clean scope to continue.
